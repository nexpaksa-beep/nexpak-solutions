H.264 vs H.265 CCTV Compression: Storage & Bandwidth Explained

Understanding video compression helps you plan CCTV storage, network bandwidth and recording performance. Learn how H.264, H.265 and newer smart-compression technologies affect your NVR system.

What Is CCTV Video Compression?

CCTV cameras generate a continuous stream of digital video. Compression reduces the amount of data that needs to be transmitted and stored while attempting to preserve useful image detail.

The codec you select can affect bitrate, storage capacity, network traffic, recorder workload and playback compatibility. It is therefore an important part of CCTV system design rather than simply a menu setting.

Key point: H.265 does not automatically mean every system will use half the storage. Real-world results depend on resolution, FPS, scene movement, bitrate settings, lighting, camera processing and the recording platform.

How H.264 Compression Works

H.264, also known as AVC, uses spatial and temporal compression. Instead of treating every frame as an entirely independent image, the encoder can reference information between frames and remove data that is redundant or less important.

For CCTV, this can substantially reduce the data rate compared with storing uncompressed video. H.264 remains common because of its maturity and wide compatibility.

How H.265 Compression Works

H.265, also known as HEVC, was developed to improve coding efficiency. It can represent video using fewer bits for a similar perceived quality under suitable conditions.

This can be useful when you are running several high-resolution IP cameras, have limited network capacity or want longer recording retention without increasing storage at the same rate.

What About H.265+ and Smart Compression?

Some CCTV manufacturers offer enhanced compression modes under names such as H.265+, smart codec or intelligent compression. These technologies can analyse relatively static surveillance scenes and reduce unnecessary data.

Performance varies by manufacturer and scene. Moving people, vehicles, rain, foliage, noise and changing lighting can all affect compression efficiency.

Compression, Resolution and FPS Work Together

Codec choice is only one part of the equation. A 4MP or 8MP camera configured at a higher frame rate can generate substantially more data than a lower-resolution camera. Similarly, a busy scene may need more bitrate than a quiet corridor.

How Compression Affects CCTV Storage

Your storage requirement is driven largely by the total bitrate of all recording streams, how long you retain them and whether recording is continuous or event-based.

For example, reducing the average bitrate of each camera can reduce the amount of data written to the NVR. But the correct approach is to choose a bitrate that still preserves the evidence you need—especially faces, number plates and other identification details.

How Compression Affects Network Bandwidth

For IP CCTV, the encoded stream travels across the network to the NVR or other recording platform. More efficient compression can therefore help reduce network traffic, particularly when several cameras operate simultaneously.

However, your switch uplinks, PoE network, NVR interfaces and remote-viewing connection still need sufficient capacity. See our CCTV bandwidth guide and CCTV network switch guide for network planning.

Does H.265 Mean Lower Image Quality?

Not necessarily. Codec efficiency and image quality are related, but codec selection alone does not determine the final result. Poor bitrate settings, excessive compression, bad lighting, motion blur and unsuitable camera positioning can all reduce usable detail.

A professional CCTV design should prioritise the surveillance objective: general monitoring, detection, recognition or identification. A camera intended to capture a plate or face at a critical entry point may require different settings from a camera covering a wide general area.

H.264 vs H.265 for Homes

For a home CCTV system, H.265 can be useful when supported by the cameras and NVR, particularly with multiple high-resolution cameras. H.264 may still be appropriate where compatibility with existing equipment is more important.

For a typical residential system, consider resolution, night performance, storage retention, remote viewing and whether the recorder supports the camera's selected codec before making the decision.

H.264 vs H.265 for Businesses

Businesses often have more cameras and longer retention requirements, making efficient video encoding valuable. Network capacity and storage planning become increasingly important as camera counts and resolutions rise.

For larger deployments, combine codec selection with a properly designed CCTV VLAN strategy, suitable switches and appropriate NVR capacity.

H.264 vs H.265 for Farms

Farms and large properties may have cameras spread over significant distances. Efficient compression can help manage network traffic, while fibre or wireless links may be required for remote camera locations.

Explore the fibre optic CCTV guide and wireless CCTV bridge guide for long-distance networking considerations.

Check NVR and Camera Compatibility

Before changing a camera from H.264 to H.265, confirm that the camera, NVR/VMS and playback software support the selected codec and profile. A camera can support a codec while an older recorder does not fully support the stream you want to use.

Also check whether a recorder's stated channel count assumes a particular resolution or bitrate. Recording capacity is not simply the number of physical camera ports.

Practical CCTV Compression Checklist

  • ✓ Confirm camera and NVR codec compatibility.
  • ✓ Set resolution according to the surveillance objective.
  • ✓ Choose an appropriate FPS rather than automatically using maximum FPS.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is better for CCTV, H.264 or H.265?

For many newer IP CCTV systems, H.265 can be attractive because it can deliver efficient encoding and reduce bitrate requirements. H.264 remains useful where broad compatibility is the priority.

How much storage can H.265 save?

There is no universal percentage. Savings depend on the camera, scene, resolution, FPS, bitrate, lighting and codec implementation. Use measured or manufacturer-provided bitrate information when sizing storage.

Can I mix H.264 and H.265 cameras?

Often, yes, but it depends on the NVR/VMS and camera compatibility. Check the recorder's supported codecs, resolutions and stream limits before deployment.

Should I always use the highest compression?

No. Excessive compression can remove useful forensic detail. Configure the system around the evidence you need and test the result in real conditions.
