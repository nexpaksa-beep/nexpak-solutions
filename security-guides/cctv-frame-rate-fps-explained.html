CCTV FPS Explained: 15FPS vs 20FPS vs 25FPS vs 30FPS

Understand frames per second, motion smoothness, storage, bandwidth and how to choose a practical CCTV frame rate for homes, businesses, farms and other properties.

What Does FPS Mean in CCTV?

FPS means frames per second. A CCTV system records a sequence of individual images to create moving video. A higher frame rate means more frames are captured or recorded in each second, which can make movement appear smoother.

Frame rate is only one part of CCTV performance. Resolution, shutter speed, lighting, lens selection, bitrate, compression, camera processing and the recorder all affect how useful recorded footage will be.

Important: A higher FPS does not automatically mean better evidence. A well-designed camera with suitable resolution, lighting and bitrate at a sensible frame rate can outperform a poorly configured high-FPS system.

When Does Higher FPS Matter?

Vehicles

Vehicle entrances and busy driveways can benefit from smoother motion, particularly where movement happens quickly through the camera's field of view.

People in Motion

Busy entrances, retail areas and access points may benefit from a higher frame rate when smooth movement and timing are important.

Active Work Areas

Warehouses, loading zones and industrial areas can have more useful footage with an appropriate frame rate and good lighting.

FPS, Resolution, Bitrate and Storage

Increasing FPS can increase the amount of video information being produced, but storage cannot be calculated from FPS alone. Resolution, codec, bitrate, scene activity, compression settings and recording schedule all matter.

For example, moving from 15FPS to 30FPS doubles the number of frames per second, but the actual storage increase depends on how the camera and recorder encode the footage.

Planning tip: Don't choose FPS in isolation. Decide what you need to identify, select suitable resolution and lens, then balance frame rate, bitrate, storage retention and network capacity.

Key factors that affect storage

  • Camera resolution
  • Frame rate
  • Video codec and compression
  • Configured bitrate
  • Scene movement and activity
  • Continuous versus motion-based recording
  • Number of cameras
  • Required retention period

Recommended CCTV FPS by Application

🏠 Homes

For general home surveillance, a moderate frame rate such as 15–20FPS can be a practical starting point. Entrances and vehicle areas may justify higher settings.

🏢 Businesses

Retail entrances, reception areas, cash-handling zones and loading areas may benefit from higher frame rates where movement and timing are important.

🌾 Farms

Large perimeter areas may not need maximum FPS everywhere. Prioritise camera placement, low-light performance, reliable connectivity and the areas where movement matters most.

FPS vs Shutter Speed: They Are Not the Same

FPS describes how many frames are captured or recorded per second. Shutter speed describes how long the camera's sensor is exposed for each frame. A high FPS setting with poor shutter-speed performance in low light can still produce blurry footage.

This is why night performance matters when designing a CCTV system. Good illumination, suitable camera sensitivity and appropriate exposure settings can be just as important as frame rate.

Common CCTV FPS Mistakes

Choosing maximum FPS everywhere

Not every camera needs the highest available frame rate. Use higher settings where the scene demands it.

Ignoring storage

Higher frame rates can affect data requirements. Confirm recorder capacity and retention targets before installation.

Ignoring lighting

FPS cannot compensate for inadequate illumination or motion blur. Night-time conditions must be part of the design.

Frequently Asked Questions

What does FPS mean on a CCTV camera?

FPS means frames per second. It describes the number of video frames captured or recorded each second.

Is 30FPS better than 15FPS?

30FPS can provide smoother motion, but it may increase data requirements. The right choice depends on the scene and evidence requirements.

What FPS is good for home CCTV?

For many general home surveillance scenes, 15–20FPS is a practical starting point. Higher rates can be useful for faster-moving areas.

Does higher FPS use more storage?

It can. However, storage depends on several variables including resolution, bitrate, codec, scene activity and recording mode.
