CCTV Network Switches Explained

What is a CCTV network switch?

A network switch connects multiple Ethernet devices so they can communicate on the same network. In an IP CCTV system, a switch can connect cameras, NVRs, computers, routers and other network equipment. A PoE switch can also deliver power to compatible cameras over Ethernet.

Connect Cameras

Bring multiple IP cameras together through Ethernet networking instead of running every camera directly to a single device.

Power over Ethernet

PoE can carry power and data on suitable Ethernet links, simplifying installation for compatible cameras.

Uplink the Network

Uplink ports connect camera switches to an NVR, core switch or wider network, depending on the system architecture.

Important: A switch is not automatically suitable for every CCTV installation. Check camera bandwidth, PoE requirements, switch capacity, cable runs and the NVR/network design before choosing equipment.

The biggest practical difference is whether the switch can provide electrical power to compatible devices through Ethernet.

Understanding PoE power budgets

A common mistake is assuming that a switch with a certain number of PoE ports can automatically power every connected camera at its maximum requirement.

Think about three limits

  • Port count: how many devices can physically connect.
  • Per-port capability: how much power an individual port can provide under the relevant PoE standard.
  • Total PoE budget: the combined power available across the switch.

For example, a switch may have enough ports for a group of cameras but still have insufficient total PoE capacity if many cameras, PTZs, illuminators or other powered devices draw significant power at the same time.

Best practice: add the expected power requirements of the connected PoE devices, allow sensible operating headroom and verify the manufacturer's specifications before installation.

CCTV bandwidth and switch capacity

Every IP camera generates network traffic. Resolution, frame rate, compression, scene activity and configured bitrate all affect the bandwidth requirement.

Camera Bitrate

Higher configured bitrates can improve video detail but increase network traffic and storage requirements.

FPS & Resolution

Higher frame rates and resolutions generally require more data than lower settings, although compression also matters.

NVR Uplink

The link from a camera switch to the NVR or core network must be sized for the traffic it carries.

Don't forget the uplink

If several cameras connect to one switch and their combined traffic travels through one uplink, that uplink becomes an important part of the design. For larger systems, the network architecture should be planned so that links are not unintentionally overloaded.

How to design a CCTV switch layout

Count the cameras

List every current camera and leave room for realistic future expansion.

Record the camera requirements

Note resolution, FPS, bitrate, PoE needs and whether any cameras have higher power demands.

Map the physical locations

Group cameras according to buildings, floors, gates, yards, warehouses or other practical zones.

Choose switch locations

Place switches where cable distances, environmental conditions, power availability and security can be managed properly.

Size uplinks and NVR connections

Make sure the traffic leaving each switch has an appropriate path to the recording system and wider network.

Test before handover

Verify camera links, PoE power, recording, remote access, network stability and fail/recovery behaviour.

Ethernet cabling matters

Even the best switch cannot compensate for unsuitable or poorly installed cabling.

  • Use appropriate Ethernet cable for the installation environment and device requirements.
  • Plan cable routes away from avoidable sources of electrical interference.
  • Protect outdoor runs against weather, physical damage and unsuitable exposure.
  • Label both ends of cables to make maintenance easier.
  • For long-distance links, consider an appropriate architecture such as fibre or suitable network extension equipment rather than exceeding the intended copper Ethernet distance.

CCTV switch setups by property type

Homes

A small PoE switch or PoE NVR may be sufficient for a straightforward home system. Keep the network secure and allow room for expansion where practical.

Businesses

Businesses may need multiple switches, structured cabling, VLANs, redundancy or centralised network management depending on scale.

Farms

Large properties can require distributed switches, long-distance links, outdoor-rated infrastructure and careful power planning between buildings.

Common CCTV switch mistakes

Buying only by port count

A 16-port switch is not automatically the right choice. Power budget and bandwidth can be just as important.

Ignoring uplink traffic

Multiple cameras can concentrate traffic onto the link between the switch and NVR or core network.

Overlooking cable distance

Network performance depends on appropriate cable, installation quality and the distance and architecture of each link.

Under-sizing PoE power

Higher-power devices can consume substantially more than basic fixed cameras, so calculate the system's power needs.

No future capacity

Leaving no spare ports or network capacity can make later camera expansion unnecessarily expensive.

Poor network security

Change default credentials, restrict unnecessary access and keep network equipment appropriately secured and maintained.

Frequently asked questions

Do IP CCTV cameras need a network switch?

Not always. Some PoE NVRs have built-in PoE ports. A separate switch becomes useful when you need additional ports, distributed camera connections, more flexible network architecture or connections between multiple network areas.

What is a PoE CCTV switch?

A PoE switch can deliver network data and electrical power over compatible Ethernet connections to supported devices such as IP cameras.

Should I use a managed or unmanaged switch?

For a small, straightforward CCTV network, an unmanaged switch may be appropriate. Larger or more advanced networks can benefit from managed features such as traffic control, VLANs and monitoring, depending on the equipment and design.

How many CCTV cameras can one switch handle?

It depends on port count, bandwidth, uplink capacity, PoE budget, camera requirements and the overall network architecture. There is no single camera number that applies to every switch.

Can a PoE switch connect to a PoE NVR?

Yes, when the devices and network architecture are compatible. The switch can provide connectivity for cameras while the NVR records the video, but the exact wiring and network configuration should be planned around the NVR's specifications.
