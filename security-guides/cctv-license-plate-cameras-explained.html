CCTV License Plate Cameras: LPR & ANPR Explained

Learn how license plate recognition cameras work, where they should be installed, what affects plate capture and how LPR/ANPR can strengthen security at homes, businesses, estates, parking areas and farms.

What Is a License Plate Camera?

A license plate camera is a CCTV camera system designed specifically to capture vehicle registration plates. These systems are commonly called LPR (License Plate Recognition) or ANPR (Automatic Number Plate Recognition) cameras.

Unlike a general overview camera, an LPR installation is planned around the plate itself: the camera angle, lens, distance, exposure, shutter behaviour, lighting, vehicle speed and background all matter.

Key point: A high-resolution camera does not automatically make a good plate camera. Correct positioning and exposure can be just as important as megapixels.

LPR vs ANPR: What's the Difference?

The terminology varies between manufacturers and security professionals. In practice, both terms are used for systems that capture vehicle plates and apply image processing or recognition to extract plate information.

TermMeaningTypical use
LPRLicense Plate RecognitionPlate capture and recognition at controlled vehicle points
ANPRAutomatic Number Plate RecognitionAutomated plate reading, logging and security workflows
General CCTVWide-area surveillancePeople, vehicles, property and incident overview

Why Normal CCTV May Struggle With Plates

A standard camera might show that a vehicle entered a driveway, but the plate can still be unreadable. Common causes include motion blur, excessive exposure from headlights, the wrong lens, a camera mounted too high, a plate occupying too few pixels, or a large viewing angle.

  • Vehicle moving too quickly for the selected exposure.
  • Headlights or sunlight causing difficult exposure conditions.
  • Camera positioned too far away from the plate.
  • Wide-angle lens used where a narrower field of view is required.
  • Camera mounted at an unsuitable vertical or horizontal angle.
  • Insufficient or poorly controlled night illumination.

Camera Positioning Is Critical

For reliable plate capture, the camera should be planned around the vehicle's path rather than simply mounted wherever there is a convenient fixing point.

Approach Lane

Position the camera so the plate occupies a useful portion of the image as vehicles approach or pass the capture point.

Viewing Angle

Avoid excessive side angles and extreme height differences. The more distorted the plate becomes, the harder reliable recognition can be.

Capture Zone

Define the exact area where the vehicle should be captured and select the lens and mounting position around that zone.

Lighting and Night-Time Plate Capture

Night-time capture can be challenging because headlights, reflective plates and dark surroundings create a difficult exposure balance. A camera designed for plate capture can use exposure controls and appropriate illumination to improve results.

Do not judge a proposed LPR system only from daytime footage. Test the actual entrance or roadway at night, especially where headlights point directly toward the camera.

Installation tip: If the site has changing light conditions, test the system during bright daylight, dusk and full darkness before finalising the mounting position.

LPR Cameras for Different Security Applications

Homes & Estates

Useful at driveway entrances, estate gates and controlled access points where vehicle movements need to be recorded.

Businesses

Useful for staff entrances, customer parking, warehouses, loading areas and vehicle access control.

Farms

Can help monitor vehicles entering long driveways, farm gates, workshops and operational areas.

LPR and Access Control

License plate recognition becomes especially useful when combined with access control. Depending on the equipment and software, recognised plates can be used as part of an automated workflow for approved or monitored vehicles.

This can complement gate automation, intercoms and conventional CCTV rather than replacing them. A strong vehicle security design often uses multiple layers: plate capture, overview CCTV, gate control and event recording.

Storage and Search

Plate capture systems generate useful evidence only if recordings are retained and can be retrieved efficiently. Storage planning should consider camera count, resolution, frame rate, compression, recording schedule and retention requirements.

LPR Installation Checklist

  1. Identify the exact vehicle capture point.
  2. Measure the expected camera-to-vehicle distance.
  3. Choose a lens suited to the capture zone.
  4. Plan the mounting height and angle.
  5. Check vehicle speed and approach direction.
  6. Test daytime, dusk and night conditions.
  7. Check headlights and reflective plate behaviour.
  8. Confirm recording and storage requirements.
  9. Integrate with access control or gate automation where appropriate.
  10. Test real vehicles before considering the installation complete.

Common LPR Mistakes

  • Choosing a camera purely because it has a high megapixel rating.
  • Using a very wide lens for a long-distance plate-reading application.
  • Mounting the camera too high above the vehicle path.
  • Ignoring headlights and night-time exposure.
  • Trying to cover too many lanes with one poorly positioned camera.
  • Failing to test the system with actual vehicles.
  • Forgetting to plan recording retention and network capacity.

How LPR Fits Into a Complete CCTV System

LPR should normally be treated as a specialised camera layer within a broader security system. General CCTV can provide wide-area context while an LPR camera focuses on vehicle identification.

A typical design might combine overview cameras, LPR cameras, an NVR, access control, gate automation, alarms and electric fencing depending on the site risk.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is an LPR camera?

An LPR camera is designed to capture vehicle registration plates clearly enough for recognition or reliable manual identification.

What is the difference between LPR and ANPR?

The terms overlap in everyday security use. Both describe systems built around vehicle plate capture and recognition.

Can a normal CCTV camera read license plates?

It can sometimes capture a readable plate under favourable conditions, but a specialised LPR design is generally better where reliable plate capture is a security requirement.

Do license plate cameras work at night?

Yes, when the camera, exposure settings, illumination and installation are appropriate for the site.
