CCTV for Businesses in South Africa

How to choose a professional CCTV security camera system for offices, retail stores, warehouses, factories, workshops and other commercial properties.

Why CCTV matters for businesses

Business CCTV is more than simply recording video. A well-planned surveillance system can help monitor entrances, protect stock and equipment, review incidents, improve visibility across a property and provide useful evidence when an event occurs.

The goal is coverage with usable detail.

A camera positioned in the wrong place can record a lot of footage without clearly showing faces, vehicles, products or activity. Commercial CCTV planning should therefore consider camera position, lighting, viewing angle, resolution, storage and the operational purpose of each camera.

What type of businesses can use CCTV?

OfficesRetailWarehousesFactoriesWorkshopsSchoolsFarmsProperty complexes

Different businesses have different surveillance priorities. A retail store may need strong coverage of entrances, tills and stock areas, while a warehouse may need long-range views across loading zones, yards and access points.

Where should business CCTV cameras be installed?

  • Main entrances and exits
  • Reception and customer access areas
  • Cashier or transaction areas where appropriate
  • Loading bays and delivery points
  • Parking areas and vehicle entrances
  • Perimeter gates and vulnerable boundary areas
  • Warehouses, stock rooms and high-value storage areas
  • Critical equipment or restricted-access zones

Camera placement should balance useful coverage, image quality, lighting, privacy and the practical limitations of the building.

HD CCTV vs IP CCTV for businesses

Both HD-over-coaxial and IP CCTV can be suitable. IP systems are particularly useful when a business wants network-based cameras, Power over Ethernet, higher-resolution options or a system that can be expanded across a larger site.

ConsiderationHD CCTVIP CCTV
InfrastructureOften uses coaxial cabling and a DVR.Uses network cabling and an NVR or network recording platform.
PowerUsually separate power plus video cabling.PoE can carry power and data over suitable network cable.

Commercial CCTV features to consider

Night vision

Outdoor business areas often require usable footage after dark. Infrared, low-light and colour-at-night technologies should be selected according to the actual lighting conditions.

Remote viewing

Modern systems can allow authorised users to view live cameras and, where supported, playback recorded footage remotely. See our CCTV remote viewing guide.

Storage

Retention requirements, camera count, resolution, frame rate, compression and recording mode all influence storage. Read the CCTV storage and hard-drive guide.

PoE networking

For IP systems, Power over Ethernet can simplify installation by allowing compatible cameras to receive power and data through network cabling.

CCTV for offices

Office surveillance commonly focuses on entrances, reception, parking, corridors, server or equipment rooms and other areas where security visibility is important. Camera placement should be designed around the building layout and legitimate security objectives.

CCTV for retail stores

Retail surveillance may focus on entrances, customer areas, tills, stock rooms, receiving areas and external access points. Image quality should be appropriate for the purpose of the camera rather than simply maximising resolution everywhere.

CCTV for warehouses and factories

Large commercial sites often require a mixture of wide-area coverage and targeted cameras. Loading bays, vehicle routes, perimeter gates, storage zones and high-value equipment may require different camera types and lenses.

Long cable runs, lighting changes, outdoor exposure and network infrastructure should all be considered before installation.

CCTV can work with other security layers

A business security system is stronger when different technologies have clearly defined roles. CCTV can provide visual verification while other systems provide detection or access control.

  • Electric fencing: perimeter deterrence and detection.
  • Alarm systems: intrusion detection and alerting.
  • Access control: control of authorised entry.
  • Gate automation: controlled vehicle and pedestrian access.
  • Intercoms: communication and visitor verification.

How much does business CCTV cost?

There is no single price for a commercial CCTV installation. The final quotation can depend on the number and type of cameras, resolution, recording equipment, storage capacity, cabling, PoE/network infrastructure, mounting requirements, site size, installation complexity and remote-access requirements.

For more detail, read CCTV installation cost in South Africa.

How to plan a commercial CCTV system

  1. Identify security risks and critical areas.
  2. Map entrances, exits, perimeter areas and internal zones.
  3. Decide what each camera must actually show.
  4. Select camera types, lenses and low-light capability.
  5. Choose HD or IP architecture based on the site and requirements.
  6. Plan recording, storage and retention.
  7. Plan network, power and cabling infrastructure.
  8. Consider remote viewing and authorised users.
  9. Integrate CCTV with other security systems where useful.
  10. Test coverage and recording after installation.

Common business CCTV mistakes

  • Choosing cameras based only on megapixels.
  • Ignoring lighting and night-time conditions.
  • Underestimating storage requirements.
  • Installing cameras where the view is obstructed.
  • Using a wide-angle camera where facial or vehicle detail is required.
  • Failing to plan cable routes and network capacity.
  • Not protecting recording equipment and network access.
  • Assuming every area needs the same camera specification.

Frequently asked questions

How many CCTV cameras does a business need?

It depends on the property layout, entrances, blind spots, valuable areas, perimeter and the level of detail required. A site assessment is the best way to determine camera positions.

Is IP CCTV good for businesses?

IP CCTV is often a strong choice for commercial properties because it supports network connectivity, PoE and scalable high-resolution camera options.

Can business CCTV be viewed remotely?

Many modern systems support secure remote viewing through a compatible mobile app or management platform when configured correctly.

Should CCTV be combined with alarms and access control?

Yes. CCTV can provide visual verification while alarms detect events and access control manages authorised entry. The technologies can complement one another.

How long should a business keep CCTV recordings?

Retention should be determined by operational needs, available storage, security requirements and applicable privacy or legal considerations. Storage planning should be done before selecting the recorder and hard drives.
