CCTV Resolution Guide

2MP vs 4MP vs 5MP vs 8MP CCTV Cameras

Choosing a CCTV camera is not simply about buying the biggest megapixel number. Resolution, lens selection, lighting, viewing distance, compression, storage and the purpose of the camera all work together.

More pixels ≠ automatically better security

The right camera is the one that delivers useful detail where you need it, with the correct lens, lighting and recording system.

What does CCTV resolution mean?

Megapixels (MP) describe the number of pixels captured by a camera's image sensor. More pixels can provide more potential detail, especially when viewing or cropping an image, but real-world performance depends on the complete camera system.

2MP

A common Full HD-class resolution. It can be suitable for many general monitoring applications when the camera and lens are correctly selected.

4MP–5MP

A useful middle ground where additional detail is desired without automatically moving to the highest resolution available.

8MP

Higher-resolution capture that can provide additional detail, particularly for larger scenes or situations where image detail matters.

Important: Resolution should be selected together with the lens, sensor quality, night performance, mounting height, viewing distance and recording requirements.

Resolution is only one part of image quality

Lens and viewing angle

A higher-resolution camera with the wrong lens can still produce poor results. A wide lens may cover more area, while a narrower lens can place more pixels on a distant subject.

Night performance

Sensor quality, infrared performance, lighting and exposure behaviour can have a major effect after dark. More megapixels cannot compensate for a poorly lit scene.

Mounting position

Camera height and angle affect the amount of useful detail captured. A badly positioned high camera may see a person without producing the best facial detail.

Compression and bitrate

Recording settings influence the final image and storage requirement. A high-resolution camera still needs appropriate bitrate and recording configuration.

How much storage does higher resolution need?

There is no single storage figure for a camera resolution because storage depends on bitrate, frame rate, compression, scene activity, recording schedule and the number of cameras.

Approximate storage is driven by: bitrate × recording time × number of cameras

Bitrate

Higher-quality streams can use more data. The appropriate bitrate depends on resolution, frame rate, codec and scene complexity.

Recording schedule

24/7 recording creates a very different storage requirement from motion-based or scheduled recording.

Number of cameras

Adding cameras multiplies the total storage requirement, making recorder and hard-drive planning important.

Planning tip: Choose the recording resolution and bitrate first, then size the recorder and surveillance hard drives around the actual number of cameras and retention target.

Choosing resolution for different properties

Homes

Prioritise entrances, driveways, gates and areas where identification matters. A well-positioned 2MP–5MP system can be practical depending on the scene.

Businesses

Retail, offices, warehouses and workshops may benefit from different resolutions at different locations. Entrances and transaction areas often need more attention to detail.

Farms

Long distances, large boundaries and low-light areas require careful lens, mounting and network planning. Resolution alone does not solve distance limitations.

When should you choose 8MP?

8MP can make sense when extra image detail is genuinely useful and the rest of the CCTV infrastructure can support it.

You need additional detail across a larger scene.
For example, a camera may need to cover a broader area while retaining useful image detail.
Your NVR or recorder supports the camera stream.
Check supported resolution, decoding capability, recording capacity and network throughput.
Your storage plan can handle the workload.
Higher-resolution cameras can increase data requirements, so calculate retention before installation.
The lens and lighting match the job.
A high-resolution sensor needs a suitable scene to turn extra pixels into useful evidence.

Common CCTV resolution mistakes

Buying only on megapixels

Two cameras with the same resolution can perform very differently because of sensor, lens, processing and low-light performance.

Ignoring the viewing distance

A camera covering a distant gate needs different optics and positioning from a camera watching a nearby doorway.

Under-sizing storage

Installing high-resolution cameras without checking recording settings and retention can result in less stored footage than expected.

Using the wrong camera type

Dome, turret, bullet and PTZ cameras have different physical and optical strengths. Select the camera around the scene, not just the megapixel rating.

Resolution vs camera type

Dome

Useful where a compact, discreet camera is preferred. Select the resolution and lens based on the actual viewing area.

Turret

Often convenient for precise positioning and maintenance. Available across multiple resolutions and feature levels.

Bullet

Useful for visible perimeter and directional coverage. Correct mounting and weather protection remain important.

A practical CCTV resolution selection checklist

Define the objective. Are you monitoring movement, identifying people, watching a gate, checking vehicles or protecting a larger area?
Measure the scene. Note approximate distances, mounting points and the area that must be visible.
Select the lens. Match focal length and field of view to the scene.
Select resolution. Choose 2MP, 4MP, 5MP, 8MP or another suitable option based on the detail required.
Plan recording. Confirm NVR/DVR compatibility, bitrate, frame rate, retention and surveillance hard-drive capacity.
Check the network. For IP/PoE systems, make sure switches, cabling and the recorder can support the cameras.

Frequently asked questions

Is 4MP better than 2MP CCTV?
4MP can capture more image detail than 2MP when the sensor, lens, lighting and scene are suitable. The best choice depends on the application rather than resolution alone.
Is 8MP CCTV worth it?
It can be worthwhile where additional detail is important, but the recorder, network, storage and lighting must all be planned for the higher-resolution stream.
Does higher CCTV resolution use more storage?
Generally, higher-resolution video can require more bitrate and storage. Compression, frame rate, scene activity and recording mode also affect the total.
What resolution is best for a home?
There is no universal answer. A properly selected 2MP, 4MP or higher camera can work well depending on viewing distance, lens, lighting, coverage and the level of detail required.
