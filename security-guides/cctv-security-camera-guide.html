What Is CCTV?

CCTV stands for closed-circuit television. A CCTV system uses cameras to capture video around a property or site and sends that footage to a recording or viewing system. Modern systems can provide live monitoring, recorded playback, motion-related events, remote access and integration with other security technologies.

Short answer: CCTV is a video surveillance system designed to monitor and record activity around a property. A typical system combines cameras, a recorder, storage, cabling or networking, power and a method of viewing live or recorded footage.

For a security installation, the objective is not simply to install as many cameras as possible. Good CCTV design starts with identifying what must be monitored, where incidents could occur, what detail is required and how footage will be used after an event.

CCTV Camera Types

Dome Cameras

Dome cameras have a compact housing and are commonly used indoors, at entrances, offices, retail areas and other locations where a discreet appearance is useful.

Bullet Cameras

Bullet cameras have a visible directional form and are often suitable for perimeter areas, driveways, gates, yards and external walls.

Turret Cameras

Turret designs can offer flexible positioning and are popular for residential and commercial installations where image quality and practical adjustment are important.

PTZ Cameras

Pan-tilt-zoom cameras can move and zoom remotely. They are useful where active monitoring of large areas is required, but they should not automatically replace strategically positioned fixed cameras.

HD CCTV vs IP CCTV

One of the most important decisions is whether an installation should use HD CCTV technology over coaxial infrastructure or IP cameras connected through a network.

FeatureHD CCTVIP CCTV
Video transmissionTypically uses coaxial cable to a DVR.Uses network cabling or suitable network infrastructure to an NVR/network system.
Resolution optionsModern HD formats can provide high-resolution images.Wide range of digital resolutions and advanced camera features.
Existing systemsCan be practical when upgrading suitable coaxial installations.Often attractive for new network-based installations.
PowerUsually requires separate power arrangements.PoE can carry power and data over suitable network cabling.
ExpansionDepends on DVR and cabling capacity.Can integrate with broader network infrastructure, subject to design.

There is no universal winner. The correct technology depends on the property, existing cabling, required features, network infrastructure, budget and future expansion plans.

CCTV Resolution and Image Quality

Resolution describes the amount of image detail a camera can capture. Common CCTV options include 2MP, 4MP, 5MP, 6MP, 8MP and 4K-class cameras, although the actual image quality depends on much more than the pixel count.

Why resolution matters

  • Higher resolution can provide more detail for identification and investigation.
  • Long viewing distances require careful lens and camera selection, not just higher megapixels.
  • Lighting conditions strongly influence real-world image quality.
  • Higher-resolution footage generally requires more storage and network bandwidth.

For entrances, gates and other identification points, camera positioning, lens selection, lighting and viewing angle should be designed together.

CCTV Night Vision Explained

Night vision allows a camera to produce usable footage in low-light or dark conditions. Many security cameras use infrared illumination, while other models use advanced low-light or colour-night technologies.

Important: A camera advertised with long infrared range does not automatically guarantee clear identification at that distance. Reflections, weather, lens choice, lighting and the subject's position can all affect the result.

For driveways, gates and perimeter areas, test the intended camera position for both daytime and nighttime conditions before finalising the installation.

DVR, NVR and CCTV Recording

What is a DVR?

A DVR, or digital video recorder, is commonly used with HD CCTV cameras that transmit video over coaxial cabling. It records footage and provides playback, configuration and other management functions.

What is an NVR?

An NVR, or network video recorder, is designed for network cameras. IP cameras send digital video across the network to the NVR, which records and manages the footage.

Why the recorder matters

The recorder is more than a box that stores video. Its capabilities can affect camera compatibility, recording resolution, playback, user access, analytics, remote viewing and storage capacity.

CCTV Storage and Hard Drives

CCTV storage requirements depend on the number of cameras, resolution, frame rate, compression, recording schedule, scene activity and how long footage needs to be retained.

  • Camera count: More cameras create more recorded data.
  • Resolution: Higher detail can increase storage requirements.
  • Recording mode: Continuous recording generally uses more storage than event-based approaches.
  • Retention: The required number of days has a major impact on drive capacity.
  • Storage hardware: CCTV systems should use storage hardware appropriate for surveillance workloads.

Storage should be calculated before installation rather than adding a drive as an afterthought.

PoE CCTV and Networking

PoE means Power over Ethernet. With compatible equipment, power and network data can travel through suitable Ethernet cabling, simplifying many IP CCTV installations.

A typical PoE system can include IP cameras, a PoE switch or NVR, network cabling, an NVR and a suitable network/router arrangement. Network design should account for cable distances, bandwidth, power budgets, environmental conditions and equipment placement.

Can CCTV Be Viewed on a Phone?

Yes. Modern CCTV systems commonly support remote viewing through a manufacturer's mobile application or web interface. Depending on the equipment, users may be able to view live cameras, search recordings, receive alerts and manage selected system functions.

Remote access should be configured securely. Use strong credentials, current firmware where supported, appropriate user permissions and secure network practices. Avoid exposing CCTV equipment unnecessarily to the public internet.

CCTV for Different Applications

Home CCTV

Residential systems commonly focus on entrances, driveways, gates, garages, passages, yards and other vulnerable areas. Camera placement should support both deterrence and useful recorded evidence.

Business CCTV

Commercial systems can monitor entrances, reception areas, cash points, stock areas, loading zones, parking areas and operational spaces while supporting incident investigation.

Farm CCTV

Farm installations can cover access roads, gates, buildings, equipment areas, yards and other strategic locations. Long distances, power availability and connectivity require careful planning.

Industrial CCTV

Industrial environments may require robust cameras, specialist mounting, wide-area coverage, access monitoring and integration with other security systems.

CCTV and Integrated Security

CCTV is strongest when it forms part of a broader security strategy. Cameras can complement electric fencing, alarm systems, access control, intercoms and gate automation.

For example, an electric fence can detect an attempted perimeter intrusion while CCTV provides visual verification. An alarm can generate an event while cameras help an operator understand what is happening. Access control can record authorised entry while CCTV provides supporting video evidence.

How Much Does CCTV Cost in South Africa?

There is no single CCTV price that applies to every property. The total cost can vary according to the number and type of cameras, resolution, recorder, storage, cabling, network equipment, installation complexity, mounting requirements, access equipment and other security integrations.

Factors that affect CCTV pricing

  • Number of cameras
  • HD or IP technology
  • Camera resolution and lens type
  • Night-vision and low-light requirements
  • DVR/NVR capacity
  • Hard-drive storage and retention requirements
  • Cabling and network infrastructure
  • Installation height and access difficulty
  • Remote-viewing and networking requirements
  • Integration with alarms, electric fencing or access control
Best approach: Define the areas that must be monitored first, then select cameras and recording equipment around those requirements. A site-specific quotation is more useful than choosing a system purely from a camera-count price.

CCTV Installation: What Should Be Planned?

  1. Identify security objectives and vulnerable areas.
  2. Mark entrances, gates, driveways and important activity zones.
  3. Choose camera types and lenses based on viewing requirements.
  4. Plan cable routes, power and network infrastructure.
  5. Calculate recorder and storage requirements.
  6. Position cameras to minimise blind spots and unwanted obstruction.
  7. Test daytime and nighttime performance.
  8. Configure recording, users and remote access securely.
  9. Document the system for future maintenance.

CCTV Maintenance

Regular maintenance helps preserve image quality and system reliability. Clean camera lenses and housings, inspect cables and connectors, check recording status, verify timestamps, review storage health and test remote access where applicable.

Outdoor cameras should also be checked for dirt, insects, moisture, vegetation growth, physical movement and environmental damage.

Frequently Asked Questions About CCTV

What is CCTV?

CCTV is a closed-circuit video surveillance system used to monitor and record activity around a property or site.

What is better: HD CCTV or IP CCTV?

Neither is automatically better for every installation. The choice depends on existing infrastructure, required features, image quality, networking, budget and future expansion.

Can CCTV cameras work at night?

Yes. Many CCTV cameras include infrared or other low-light technologies. Actual nighttime performance depends on the camera, lens, lighting, environment and installation position.

How many CCTV cameras do I need?

The required number depends on the property layout and security objectives. Cameras should be positioned around important risk areas and designed to provide useful coverage rather than simply selecting a fixed number.

Can I watch my CCTV from my phone?

Many modern systems support secure mobile remote viewing, provided the recorder/cameras and network are correctly configured.

How long should CCTV footage be stored?

Retention requirements vary by application. Storage capacity should be calculated from camera count, resolution, recording mode and the desired retention period.

Does CCTV prevent crime?

CCTV can act as a visible deterrent and provide valuable evidence, but it should form part of a broader security strategy rather than being treated as a guarantee against crime.

How much does CCTV installation cost in South Africa?

Pricing varies significantly according to equipment, camera count, cabling, storage, installation complexity and site requirements. A property-specific assessment gives a more reliable figure.
