Magnetic Door & Window Alarm Sensors

How magnetic contacts work, where they should be installed, wired vs wireless options, common faults and how door and window sensors fit into a properly designed alarm system.

Get a Security QuoteRead the Alarm Systems Guide

What is a magnetic alarm contact?

A magnetic door or window contact is a perimeter security sensor designed to detect the opening or closing of a protected access point. A typical contact uses two parts: a magnet mounted on the moving section and a reed switch or electronic sensing element mounted on the frame.

Simple principle: when the protected opening changes position, the relationship between the magnet and sensor changes. The alarm panel can then interpret the zone condition and respond according to the programmed security configuration.

Doors

Protect exterior doors, pedestrian entrances, security doors, store rooms and other important access points.

Windows

Suitable opening windows can be protected where the contact can be securely installed and correctly aligned.

Commercial openings

Contacts can help monitor selected doors, cabinets, service entrances and other controlled openings in business environments.

Perimeter protection

Magnetic contacts complement motion detection by providing a direct opening-point detection layer.

How magnetic door and window sensors work

In a conventional magnetic contact, the magnet and reed switch are positioned close together when the door or window is closed. Opening the protected point separates the two components and changes the electrical state of the contact.

ConditionTypical sensor stateAlarm system interpretation
Opening closedMagnet aligned with contactZone generally remains normal
Opening movedMagnet separates from contactZone can change state and trigger an alarm when armed
Contact damaged or wiring faultDepends on circuit designMay create a trouble condition or alarm

Exact zone behaviour depends on the alarm panel, contact type, end-of-line configuration and programming. A qualified installer should configure and test the complete circuit.

Wired vs wireless magnetic contacts

ConnectionCable to the alarm system or zone expanderRadio communication with a compatible receiver/panel
InstallationMay require cable routing and access to the alarm wiringOften easier where new cabling is difficult
PowerUsually supplied through the alarm circuit/designNormally uses a replaceable battery
MaintenanceInspect cable, terminals and physical mountingIncludes battery condition and wireless communication
Best fitNew builds, renovations or accessible cable routesExisting buildings where minimising new cabling is important
There is no universal winner. The best option depends on the building, alarm platform, installation conditions, desired expansion and overall security design.

Where should magnetic contacts be installed?

Sensor placement should be based on the actual threat model and the way the opening operates. Exterior doors are usually high-priority perimeter points, while selected windows may be protected according to accessibility and risk.

  • Prioritise accessible exterior doors and vulnerable openings.
  • Mount the two sensor components securely so normal movement does not cause unreliable operation.
  • Follow the contact manufacturer's permitted alignment and separation distance.
  • Protect wiring from mechanical damage, tampering and accidental disconnection.
  • Consider sliding doors and windows separately because their movement can require different mounting arrangements.
  • Test every protected opening with the alarm panel after installation.

Magnetic contacts for different properties

Homes

Useful for front and back doors, garage access, selected windows and other perimeter points where opening detection is valuable.

Businesses

Can protect staff entrances, offices, storerooms, service doors and selected high-risk openings as part of a zoned alarm system.

Farms & outbuildings

Can be incorporated into suitable building alarms for offices, workshops, equipment rooms and other protected structures.

Common magnetic contact problems

False alarms

Check alignment, mounting, excessive movement, damaged wiring and zone configuration. Wireless contacts should also be checked for battery and communication issues.

Door shows open

The magnet may be too far from the sensor, misaligned, loose or incorrectly positioned. The zone and wiring should also be tested.

Intermittent faults

Movement, loose terminals, damaged cable, poor mounting or marginal wireless communication can produce intermittent conditions.

Wireless battery warning

Replace the battery with the correct type and follow the alarm manufacturer's procedure for maintaining and testing the sensor.

Security design considerations

Magnetic contacts are one component of a layered security system. For higher-risk properties, they can be combined with motion detection, CCTV, electric fencing, access control, panic devices, sirens and appropriate monitoring.

Important: a door contact tells the system that a protected opening has changed state. It does not by itself determine who opened the door or prevent forced entry. Layered security and correct system design remain essential.

Installation checklist

  • Identify priority doors and windows based on the property's risk profile.
  • Confirm the alarm panel supports the selected contact technology.
  • Choose wired or wireless based on the building and installation route.
  • Use compatible contacts and manufacturer-approved installation methods.
  • Secure both contact components and verify their permitted operating gap.
  • Check cable protection or wireless signal quality.
  • Configure the correct alarm zone and end-of-line arrangement where applicable.
  • Test opening, closing, arming and alarm conditions before handover.
  • Document the protected zones so future maintenance is easier.

Frequently asked questions

What is a magnetic door alarm sensor?A magnetic contact uses a magnet and sensing switch to detect a change in the position of a protected door, window or similar opening.
Can magnetic contacts be wireless?Yes. Wireless versions are available for compatible alarm platforms and are particularly useful where installing new cable is difficult.
Can they protect sliding doors?Yes, provided the selected contact and mounting arrangement are suitable for the sliding door's movement and installation conditions.
Do magnetic contacts replace PIR sensors?No. They serve different purposes. Contacts detect opening-point changes, while PIRs are generally used for movement detection in a protected area.
How often should magnetic contacts be tested?They should be included in routine alarm-system testing and checked whenever a door/window, sensor, wiring route or alarm zone is serviced.
