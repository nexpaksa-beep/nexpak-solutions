Best CCTV Camera Types: Dome, Bullet, Turret & PTZ Explained

Choosing a CCTV camera is not simply about buying the highest resolution available. The right camera depends on where it will be installed, what you need to see, the lighting conditions, viewing distance, lens angle and how the system will be recorded and monitored.

Quick answer: There is no single “best” CCTV camera for every property. Turret, dome, bullet and PTZ cameras each have different strengths. For a home, a combination of camera types may provide better coverage than using one type everywhere. Choose cameras according to viewing distance, field of view, lighting, mounting location, weather exposure and the security objective.

Which CCTV camera type should you choose?

The most common CCTV form factors used in residential, commercial, industrial and agricultural security are dome, turret, bullet and PTZ cameras. Network/IP cameras and HD-over-coax cameras can use several of these physical designs, so “camera type” and “camera technology” are not always the same thing.

Dome cameras

Compact cameras with a rounded housing. Useful for entrances, indoor areas and locations where a discreet installation is preferred.

Turret cameras

Flexible camera assemblies that are popular for homes and businesses because the lens can often be positioned easily during installation.

Bullet cameras

Directional cameras commonly used for driveways, boundaries, gates and areas where a longer or clearly defined viewing direction is needed.

PTZ cameras

Pan-tilt-zoom cameras designed for active control and wider-area monitoring, particularly on larger sites.

1. Dome CCTV cameras

Dome cameras are named for their rounded housing. They are commonly installed on ceilings, soffits and walls where a compact security camera is desirable. Depending on the model, a dome may be suitable for indoor or outdoor use.

When is a dome camera a good choice?

  • Indoor entrances, reception areas and corridors.
  • Residential installations where a discreet appearance is preferred.
  • Locations where the camera should blend into the building.
  • Ceiling or wall installations with suitable mounting hardware.

For outdoor use, check the camera’s weather-resistance rating and make sure the selected lens and infrared system suit the location.

2. Turret CCTV cameras

Turret cameras are one of the most versatile choices for modern CCTV installations. Their lens assembly can generally be positioned during installation, making it easier to fine-tune the viewing direction.

Why are turret cameras popular?

They can be a practical choice for front doors, driveways, yards, shops, offices and many outdoor walls. They are also useful where installers need straightforward access to the camera and lens for alignment and maintenance.

Tip: Camera form factor matters, but the specification matters more. A high-quality turret camera with the wrong lens or poor placement can perform worse than a correctly selected lower-resolution camera.

3. Bullet CCTV cameras

Bullet cameras have a longer, directional housing and are often used when the camera is intended to monitor a particular zone. They are common around gates, driveways, parking areas and property boundaries.

Why use a bullet camera?

  • Clear directional viewing.
  • Useful for driveways and approach routes.
  • Can be effective for perimeter monitoring.
  • The visible camera can contribute to a security deterrent strategy.

Mounting height, angle and lighting are critical. Avoid pointing the camera directly toward strong lights, reflective surfaces or the rising/setting sun where possible.

4. PTZ CCTV cameras

PTZ stands for pan, tilt and zoom. A PTZ camera can move horizontally and vertically and use optical zoom to inspect a distant area. Some models support automatic patrols, presets and tracking features.

Where are PTZ cameras useful?

PTZ cameras can be valuable on larger commercial properties, industrial sites, yards, parking areas and selected farm applications. They are particularly useful when an operator needs to actively inspect different parts of a large scene.

Important: A PTZ has a limitation: when it is looking in one direction, it is not simultaneously looking in another. For critical perimeter coverage, fixed cameras can provide continuous coverage while the PTZ adds active verification and zoom capability.

Fixed cameras vs PTZ cameras

For many security installations, the best approach is not “fixed cameras or PTZ”. It is a combination. Fixed cameras can provide consistent coverage of important points such as gates, doors and boundaries, while a PTZ can be used for wider-area observation and investigation.

Fixed cameras

Always watch their assigned field of view. Excellent for reliable coverage of specific security zones.

PTZ cameras

Can move and zoom to inspect different areas, but coverage changes as the camera moves.

Camera type is only part of the decision

Before selecting a CCTV camera, evaluate the technical specification and installation environment.

Resolution

Higher resolution can provide more detail, but it also increases storage and bandwidth requirements. Choose resolution according to the identification task rather than simply selecting the largest number.

Lens and field of view

A wide-angle lens can cover more area but may show less detail at distance. A narrower lens can provide more detail on a specific target area. Lens choice is especially important for gates, entrances and long driveways.

Night performance

Check infrared performance, low-light sensitivity, image processing and whether visible lighting is available. Read our CCTV night vision guide for a deeper explanation.

Weather protection

Outdoor cameras should be selected for the environmental conditions in which they will operate. Do not assume every camera is suitable for rain, dust, heat or direct exposure.

Recording technology

IP cameras commonly connect to an NVR or network infrastructure, while HD-over-coax cameras commonly connect to DVR equipment. Read our HD CCTV vs IP CCTV guide for the main differences.

CCTV camera placement matters more than camera count

Installing more cameras does not automatically create better security. Cameras should be positioned around meaningful security zones: entrances, gates, driveways, parking areas, loading points, valuable equipment and vulnerable perimeter sections.

  • Identify what must be detected, recognised or identified.
  • Measure the distance to the target.
  • Choose a lens that matches that distance.
  • Check daytime and nighttime lighting.
  • Avoid blind spots and unnecessary overlapping views.
  • Protect cabling and mounting hardware.
  • Confirm recording, storage and remote-viewing requirements.

How many CCTV cameras does a property need?

There is no universal number. A small home might need only a few strategically placed cameras, while a business, warehouse or farm may require a larger system. The correct design depends on the property layout, risk areas, viewing objectives and desired level of coverage.

Start with the security objectives rather than a camera count. A professional site assessment can then determine camera locations, lens requirements, recorder capacity, storage and networking.

Camera type and CCTV storage

The camera type influences the system design, but storage depends heavily on the number of cameras, resolution, frame rate, bitrate, compression, recording schedule and retention period.

Choosing CCTV for homes, businesses and farms

🏠 Homes

Prioritise entrances, gates, driveways and areas where an intruder could approach the property. Turret and dome cameras are often versatile, with bullet cameras useful for directional driveway or boundary views.

🏢 Businesses

Consider entrances, tills or transaction areas where appropriate, stock areas, loading zones, parking and perimeter access. Commercial installations often benefit from careful recording and user-access planning.

🚜 Farms

Long distances, power availability, connectivity and environmental exposure become more important. Camera selection may include long-range fixed cameras, PTZ cameras and remote monitoring solutions.

Integrating CCTV with other security systems

CCTV is strongest when it forms part of a layered security strategy. Depending on the property, cameras can complement electric fencing, alarms, access control, intercoms and gate automation.

Common CCTV camera selection mistakes

  1. Choosing only by megapixels: Resolution does not fix poor lens selection or bad positioning.
  2. Ignoring nighttime conditions: A camera that looks excellent in daylight may struggle at night if the scene is poorly lit.
  3. Using a wide lens for every location: Wide coverage can reduce useful detail at distance.
  4. Relying on a PTZ alone: A moving camera cannot continuously watch every direction.
  5. Ignoring storage: More cameras and higher recording quality can require substantially more storage.
  6. Poor installation: Glare, vibration, water exposure, cable damage and blind spots can undermine a good camera.

Frequently asked questions

What type of CCTV camera is best for a home?

There is no universal best type. Turret and dome cameras are versatile for many residential locations, while bullet cameras can be useful for driveways and boundaries. The correct choice depends on the location, lighting, viewing distance and required detail.

What is the difference between dome and bullet CCTV?

Dome cameras have a compact rounded housing and are often chosen for discreet installations. Bullet cameras have a directional housing and are commonly used for driveways, gates and perimeter views.

Are turret cameras better than dome cameras?

Not universally. Turret cameras can be convenient to position and maintain, while dome cameras can offer a compact, discreet installation. The site and camera specification should determine the choice.

What is a PTZ camera?

PTZ means pan, tilt and zoom. The camera can move horizontally and vertically and use optical zoom. PTZ cameras are useful for active monitoring of larger areas.

How do I choose the right CCTV camera?

Start with the security objective and target distance. Then consider camera type, lens, resolution, night performance, weather protection, mounting position, recording system, storage, connectivity and remote viewing.
