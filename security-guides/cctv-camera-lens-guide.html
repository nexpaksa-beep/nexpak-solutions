CCTV Camera Lens Guide: 2.8mm vs 3.6mm vs 6mm vs 12mm

Choosing the right CCTV camera is not only about megapixels. The lens determines how wide the scene appears, how much of an area you can cover and how large a distant subject appears in the recorded image.

A CCTV camera with the wrong lens can produce a technically sharp image that still fails to show the detail you need. Understanding focal length helps you match a camera to entrances, corridors, yards, driveways, perimeter areas and large commercial sites.

Key idea: Lower focal lengths generally give a wider view. Higher focal lengths generally give a narrower view and are useful when you need to concentrate on a more distant area.

What Does CCTV Lens Size Mean?

CCTV lens size is commonly expressed in millimetres (mm), referring to focal length. In practical terms, focal length affects the camera's field of view and how subjects are framed.

2.8mmWide coverage; useful where you need to see more of the surrounding area.
3.6mmA useful middle ground for many general-purpose installations.
6mm+Narrower coverage; useful for concentrating on longer or more distant scenes.

2.8mm vs 3.6mm vs 6mm vs 12mm

LensTypical viewing characterUseful applicationsMain trade-off

Exact field-of-view angles vary by sensor size and camera design, so lens millimetres should not be treated as a universal viewing-distance chart.

Field of View: The Most Important Concept

Imagine standing in the middle of a room. A wide lens lets the camera see more of the room from that position. A narrower lens sees a smaller section, but that section occupies more of the image.

Wide lens

  • Captures more scene width.
  • Useful when the camera is relatively close to the area being monitored.
  • Can reduce the need for multiple cameras in some layouts.

Narrow lens

  • Concentrates the image on a smaller area.
  • Can be useful for gates, driveways and distant targets.
  • Requires more careful aiming because small positioning errors can leave important areas outside the frame.

Choosing a Lens for Different Locations

🏠 Homes

For entrances, patios and general yard coverage, a wider lens can be useful. For a long driveway or a gate positioned farther from the camera, a narrower lens may be more appropriate.

🏢 Businesses

Retail entrances and open areas may benefit from broader coverage, while loading zones, cash-office approaches, gates and specific corridors may require a more targeted view.

🌾 Farms and large properties

Large properties often need a mixture of lenses. Wide views can monitor buildings and activity areas, while narrower lenses can focus on gates, access roads and specific perimeter points.

Lens Choice and Identification

A wider image is not automatically better for identification. If a person is very small in the frame, increasing camera resolution alone may not solve the problem. Camera height, distance, lighting, lens selection, sensor performance and the subject's movement all matter.

Security planning tip: Decide what you need to see before selecting the camera. “Monitor the driveway” and “identify a person at the gate” are different objectives and may require different camera positioning and lens choices.

Fixed vs Varifocal CCTV Lenses

A fixed lens has a set focal length. A varifocal camera allows the lens to be adjusted across a focal-length range during installation. This can make it easier to fine-tune the view when the exact framing is uncertain.

Varifocal cameras can be particularly useful where a site needs precise coverage of a gate, driveway, entrance or perimeter section.

Common CCTV Lens Mistakes

  • Choosing a camera based only on megapixels.
  • Using a very wide lens when the real objective is distant identification.
  • Using a narrow lens where broad situational awareness is required.
  • Ignoring mounting height and camera-to-subject distance.
  • Failing to test the image at night.
  • Assuming the same lens will work equally well at every property.

How Lens Choice Fits Into a Complete CCTV System

The lens is only one part of the design. You should also consider camera type, resolution, night performance, recording hardware, storage, network architecture and remote viewing.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the difference between a 2.8mm and 6mm CCTV lens?

A 2.8mm lens generally provides a wider field of view, while a 6mm lens gives a narrower view and concentrates more of the image on a particular area.

Is 2.8mm good for a home CCTV camera?

It can be a good choice for broad coverage, especially where the camera is close to the area being monitored. The correct choice still depends on the property layout.

Is a higher mm CCTV lens always better?

No. Higher focal lengths are narrower. They can be useful for distant or targeted areas but are not ideal when you need a very wide scene.

What lens is best for a long driveway?

A 6mm or 12mm lens may be useful for a long driveway when the objective is a more targeted view, but the exact choice should be based on distance, required coverage and mounting position.
