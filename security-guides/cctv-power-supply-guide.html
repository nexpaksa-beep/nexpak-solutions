CCTV Power Supply Guide

12V • PoE • UPS • Backup Power • Voltage Drop
Reliable CCTV starts with reliable power. Learn how 12V DC supplies, Power over Ethernet, UPS systems and backup power work — and how to plan a CCTV installation that stays dependable.

Power is part of the security system

A camera can have excellent resolution and night vision, but if its power delivery is poorly designed, the system can become unreliable.

Why CCTV Power Matters

CCTV equipment needs stable electrical power for cameras, infrared illumination, recorders, switches, network equipment and displays. The correct power architecture depends on the camera type, cable distance, equipment load and whether you need operation during power interruptions.

Camera Power

Traditional cameras may use local low-voltage DC power, while many IP cameras can receive power over Ethernet.

NVR & DVR Power

Recording equipment must remain powered if you want continuous recording during a mains interruption.

Network Equipment

PoE switches, routers and other networking equipment may also need backup power for remote access and IP camera operation.

12V CCTV Power vs PoE

The two approaches are common in different CCTV architectures. The best choice depends on whether the cameras are traditional wired cameras or network/IP cameras and how the system is being installed.

What Is PoE CCTV?

Power over Ethernet allows compatible IP cameras to receive electrical power and network data through Ethernet cabling. This can make an IP CCTV installation cleaner and easier to manage, particularly when cameras are connected to a PoE switch or PoE NVR.

One Network Connection

A compatible Ethernet run can provide both network connectivity and power to the camera.

Centralised Backup

Backing up the PoE switch and NVR can provide a central way to keep multiple cameras operating during an outage.

Check the Power Budget

The switch must have enough available PoE capacity for the connected cameras and their actual operating requirements.

CCTV UPS & Backup Power

A CCTV UPS can help protect recording and surveillance equipment from short power interruptions and provide temporary runtime when mains power is unavailable. Backup planning should include the complete equipment chain rather than the cameras alone.

UPS Runtime

Runtime depends on the UPS capacity, battery condition and total connected load. More equipment generally means shorter runtime.

Load Calculation

Include the NVR/DVR, PoE switch, cameras, router, fibre equipment and other essential devices that must remain online.

Maintenance

UPS batteries age. Test backup runtime periodically and replace batteries or equipment according to the manufacturer's requirements.

Load-shedding planning: If continuous surveillance is important, consider the entire power path from the mains supply through the backup system to the cameras and recorder. A backup battery that powers only the NVR will not keep cameras recording if their camera or PoE power source shuts down.

Voltage Drop & Long CCTV Cable Runs

Voltage drop can become an issue when power travels over long cable runs. The longer the run and the greater the current demand, the more important correct cable selection and power architecture become.

Measure the distance

Know the approximate cable length between the power source and each device. Do not design only from a floor plan without considering the actual cable route.

Check the device load

Use the manufacturer's electrical specifications and consider additional consumption from features such as IR illumination or heaters where applicable.

Select the correct distribution method

For longer distances, a properly designed PoE, local-power or distributed-power architecture may be more suitable than simply extending a small power cable.

Test under real conditions

Verify the system after installation, including night operation, when some cameras may have higher power demand.

How to Plan CCTV Power Properly

List every camera and its required power.
Identify whether each camera uses DC power or PoE.
Calculate the expected PoE switch power budget where applicable.
Account for NVR/DVR and network equipment.
Consider cable length and voltage drop.
Decide how long the system must operate during an outage.
Place critical equipment on appropriate backup power.
Test daytime and nighttime operation.

CCTV Power for Different Properties

Homes

For a home CCTV system, a central NVR/PoE setup with correctly sized backup power can simplify the installation and help maintain recording during short outages.

Businesses

Businesses may need longer runtime, more cameras and additional network equipment. Power budgets and UPS capacity become increasingly important as systems scale.

Farms

Large properties may have long cable runs and remote camera locations. Fibre, suitable network architecture, local power solutions and solar/backup strategies may need to be considered.

Common CCTV Power Problems

Camera repeatedly reboots

Possible causes include inadequate power delivery, a poor connection, excessive cable losses or an overloaded power source.

Night image becomes unstable

Infrared illumination can change a camera's power demand. A marginal power system may become unreliable when night mode activates.

Some PoE cameras work, others do not

The PoE switch may have an insufficient power budget, or a particular cable/device combination may have an issue.

NVR stays on but cameras disappear

The recorder may be receiving backup power while the PoE switch or camera power source is not. Backup the complete surveillance path.

UPS runtime is shorter than expected

Connected load, battery age, battery condition and the UPS's actual usable capacity all affect runtime.

Intermittent faults

Loose connectors, damaged cables, unsuitable power supplies and environmental conditions can create faults that appear random.

CCTV Power Supply FAQs

Do CCTV cameras need 12V power?

Many traditional CCTV cameras use low-voltage DC power, commonly 12V, but the exact requirement depends on the camera. Many IP cameras can instead receive power through PoE.

What is PoE CCTV?

PoE CCTV uses Power over Ethernet so compatible network cameras can receive electrical power and data through Ethernet infrastructure.

Will a UPS keep CCTV working during load shedding?

A correctly sized UPS can keep supported CCTV equipment operating during an outage for a limited period. Runtime depends on equipment load, UPS capacity and battery condition.

Why does a CCTV camera lose power at the end of a cable?

Long cable runs can create voltage drop. The installation should account for cable length, current demand and the chosen power-distribution method.

Should the NVR and PoE switch be on backup power?

If continued recording and IP camera operation are required during a power interruption, the critical components of the CCTV and network path should be included in the backup-power design.

Can I use any power supply with a CCTV camera?

No. Use a power source that matches the camera's required voltage, polarity, current capacity and connector requirements, or use a compatible PoE standard where applicable.
