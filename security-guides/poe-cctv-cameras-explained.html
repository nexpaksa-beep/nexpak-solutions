PoE CCTV Cameras Explained

Understand how Power over Ethernet CCTV works, how PoE cameras connect to NVRs and switches, what cabling they need, how power budgets work, and how to plan a reliable IP CCTV installation in South Africa.

Request a Quote Explore CCTV
Quick answer: PoE, or Power over Ethernet, allows a compatible IP CCTV camera to receive power and send video/data through the same Ethernet cable. A PoE NVR or PoE network switch supplies power while the network connection carries camera traffic. This can simplify cabling, installation and system management.

What is a PoE CCTV camera?

A PoE CCTV camera is a network/IP camera designed to use Ethernet infrastructure for both communication and electrical power. Instead of running a separate power cable to every camera, a suitable Ethernet connection can provide the camera with power while carrying its video and network data.

For many modern CCTV installations, this creates a clean architecture: cameras connect by Ethernet to a PoE NVR or PoE switch, the recorder stores footage, and the system can provide local and remote viewing when configured for it.

How does PoE CCTV work?

1. Power

The PoE equipment supplies electrical power through compatible Ethernet cabling to the camera.

2. Network data

The same cable carries network traffic between the camera and the NVR or switch.

3. Recording

The NVR receives camera streams and records them to its installed storage according to the configured recording settings.

4. Viewing

Users can view cameras locally and, where properly configured, remotely through compatible software or mobile applications.

Important: The camera, switch/NVR and cabling should be compatible with the required PoE standard and power demand. Do not assume that every Ethernet port provides PoE.

PoE power standards and power budgets

PoE equipment is designed around defined power-delivery standards. In a CCTV project, the important practical question is not simply whether a port says “PoE”, but whether the equipment can supply enough power for the connected camera under its expected operating conditions.

Standard cameras may use relatively modest power, while devices with powerful infrared illumination, heaters, microphones, speakers, wipers or PTZ motors can have higher requirements. A switch or NVR therefore has a total PoE power budget that should be checked before cameras are connected.

  • Check the camera's stated power requirement.
  • Check the NVR or switch's per-port capability.
  • Check the total PoE budget across all active ports.
  • Allow sensible capacity rather than designing permanently at the limit.

What Ethernet cable should be used?

Cat5e and Cat6 Ethernet cable are common choices for IP CCTV. Cat6 can offer greater performance headroom and is often selected for new structured cabling installations. The correct cable depends on the required network performance, distance, environment and installation method.

Distance matters

Typical Ethernet links have a practical copper run limit around 100 metres including patching. If cameras are farther away, a professional design may use suitable network extensions, fibre, additional network equipment or another appropriate architecture rather than simply exceeding the cable specification.

Outdoor cabling

Outdoor camera runs should be planned for the environment. Consider UV exposure, moisture, lightning risk, physical protection, conduit, termination quality and separation from sources of electrical interference. Where a site has long outdoor runs, a proper network and electrical protection strategy is important.

PoE CCTV bandwidth

PoE solves power delivery, but it does not eliminate the need to plan network bandwidth. Each camera produces a video stream. Resolution, frame rate, compression, scene complexity and camera configuration influence the bitrate.

For example, a system with several high-resolution cameras can create substantially more traffic than a small installation using lower-resolution streams. The switch uplinks and NVR network interface therefore need to handle the aggregate traffic without becoming a bottleneck.

PoE CCTV installation example

Small home

Outdoor cameras can connect to a compact PoE NVR. The NVR records locally and can provide remote viewing when the network and account configuration are correctly set up.

Business premises

A PoE switch can distribute cameras across a structured network while the recorder is located securely in an equipment area.

Warehouse

Long cable routes, high mounting positions and difficult environments may require careful network design, surge protection and appropriate access equipment.

Farm or large property

Long distances may make fibre, wireless links, solar power or other network-extension methods part of the overall CCTV design.

Advantages of PoE CCTV

  • Simpler cabling: one Ethernet cable can handle power and network communication.
  • Centralised power: cameras can be powered from a PoE NVR or switch.
  • Easy expansion: additional cameras can be added when spare ports and power/network capacity exist.
  • Professional installation: structured Ethernet cabling can produce a clean, serviceable installation.
  • Reliable wired connection: Ethernet avoids many of the connection variables associated with wireless cameras.
  • Central recording: cameras can feed a dedicated NVR for local footage storage.

Common PoE CCTV problems

Camera has no power

Check the Ethernet connection, PoE port status, cable termination and the available power budget. A camera with unusually high power demand may exceed what the port can deliver.

Camera powers on but has no video

Check network configuration, link status, camera compatibility, IP addressing and the NVR's camera configuration. Faulty or incorrectly terminated cabling can also cause intermittent or failed communication.

Camera keeps disconnecting

Look for damaged cables, poor terminations, inadequate PoE capacity, network loops, unstable switches or environmental issues. Test the camera and cable systematically rather than replacing equipment at random.

Night vision causes problems

Some cameras draw more power when infrared illumination activates. If a system is marginally powered, problems may become more noticeable at night. Check the camera's maximum power requirement and the PoE budget.

PoE CCTV security considerations

A wired camera system is only one part of a secure installation. Protect the recorder, network equipment and camera cabling from unauthorised access. Use strong account credentials, keep compatible firmware and applications maintained, and separate security devices from unnecessary network exposure where practical.

For remote viewing, use the manufacturer's supported secure access method and avoid exposing network services unnecessarily. Professional configuration should take account of the customer's network, cybersecurity requirements and operational needs.

PoE CCTV for homes, businesses and farms

PoE is flexible enough for residential, commercial, industrial and agricultural surveillance. The right design depends on camera positions, viewing objectives, cable distances, lighting, storage requirements, network infrastructure and the environment.

For a home, a few strategically positioned cameras may cover entrances, driveways and important external areas. For a business, the design may include multiple entrances, tills, stock areas, loading zones, parking and perimeter views. Farms and large properties may require a combination of wired and extended network links.

How to plan a PoE CCTV system

  1. Identify what areas need to be observed.
  2. Select the appropriate camera type, resolution and lens for each location.
  3. Determine the required recording period and storage capacity.
  4. Map cable routes and calculate approximate distances.
  5. Select a compatible PoE NVR or PoE switch.
  6. Calculate both per-port and total PoE power requirements.
  7. Check network bandwidth and uplink capacity.
  8. Plan surge, environmental and physical protection where appropriate.
  9. Configure recording, user access, time settings and remote viewing.
  10. Test every camera during both daytime and nighttime conditions.

Frequently asked questions

What is PoE in CCTV?

PoE means Power over Ethernet. It allows compatible network equipment to deliver electrical power and network connectivity over Ethernet cabling.

Can PoE cameras work without internet?

Yes. Local camera-to-NVR recording can work without internet access. Internet connectivity is generally needed for remote access and cloud-connected features.

Can I use Cat6 cable for PoE cameras?

Yes. Cat6 is commonly used for PoE IP CCTV installations when properly terminated and installed.

How far can a PoE CCTV camera be from the NVR?

A standard copper Ethernet link is commonly designed around a 100-metre channel limit. Longer routes need a suitable extension or network architecture.

Do all IP cameras use PoE?

No. Some IP cameras use separate power supplies or other power methods. Always check the camera specification and the NVR or switch compatibility.

Is PoE better than Wi-Fi CCTV?

Neither is automatically better for every property. PoE is often advantageous for reliable, structured multi-camera installations, while Wi-Fi can be useful where Ethernet cabling is difficult.
