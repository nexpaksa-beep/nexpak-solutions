PIR Motion Sensors for Alarm Systems

How PIR motion detectors work, where they should be installed, how pet immunity and outdoor detection work, what causes false alarms and how to choose the right motion sensor for a South African security system.

A PIR (Passive Infrared) sensor is one of the most common detection devices used with intrusion alarm systems. It is designed to identify changes in infrared energy across its detection field and report an event to the alarm panel. The quality of the final result depends just as much on detector selection and placement as it does on the alarm panel itself.

What does PIR mean?

PIR stands for Passive Infrared. “Passive” means the detector does not actively transmit a signal to measure its surroundings in the way some other sensing technologies do. Instead, it monitors infrared radiation and looks for changes consistent with movement.

Why PIRs matter

Door contacts protect specific openings, while PIRs can protect an entire room or movement zone. A well-designed alarm can combine both technologies so an intruder may be detected at the perimeter or after entering an internal area.

How a PIR motion sensor works

A typical PIR detector uses a pyroelectric sensing element together with an optical system, commonly a Fresnel lens, to divide the surrounding area into detection zones. When a warm object such as a person moves across those zones, the infrared pattern reaching the sensor changes.

Important: A PIR does not simply “see a person” like a camera. It detects changes in infrared energy. That is why installation conditions, temperature, movement direction and the surrounding environment affect performance.

Where should PIR sensors be installed?

There is no single height or position that works for every detector. The manufacturer's instructions should always take priority. In general, installers consider the room shape, likely movement paths, furniture, doors and windows, heat sources and the detector's specified coverage.

  • Cover likely paths into or through the protected area.
  • Avoid aiming directly at heaters, air-conditioning outlets or other unstable heat sources.
  • Keep the detector firmly mounted and free from objects that can move in its field of view.
  • Consider doors and windows so the detector complements, rather than duplicates, perimeter protection.
  • For outdoor detectors, follow the specific environmental and mounting requirements of the product.

Pet-friendly PIR sensors

Pet immunity is not a universal guarantee against every animal or every installation condition. Different detectors have different specifications and limitations. A pet-friendly system should be designed around the actual animal, its size, movement patterns and the rooms it occupies.

Where practical, perimeter contacts and carefully selected detection zones can complement pet-friendly motion detectors and reduce dependence on a single sensor type.

Why PIR sensors cause false alarms

Environmental causes

  • Rapid temperature changes
  • Direct sunlight or reflections
  • Air movement from HVAC systems
  • Moving curtains, plants or hanging objects
  • Insects or small animals near the detector

Installation causes

  • Poor detector position
  • Loose mounting
  • Incorrect coverage assumptions
  • Unsuitable detector for the environment
  • Incorrect settings or configuration

Indoor PIR vs outdoor PIR

Indoor PIRs normally operate in a comparatively controlled environment. Outdoor detection is more challenging because wind, rain, sunlight, vegetation, insects, temperature changes and animals can all affect a detector's environment.

For external protection, use equipment specifically designed for outdoor use and consider whether another technology—such as beams, perimeter detection, CCTV analytics or electric fencing—would provide a more appropriate layer.

PIR sensors and the alarm panel

A PIR normally connects to an alarm control panel through a wired zone or, with compatible equipment, through a wireless transmitter. The panel interprets the detector event according to the programmed zone and system state.

Good system design considers what should happen when the sensor is triggered, how zones are named, whether the property is occupied, and what communication or monitoring path is available.

PIRs in a layered security system

PIR motion detection works best as one layer rather than the only layer. A residential or commercial security system can combine:

  • Door and window magnetic contacts
  • PIR or dual-technology motion detectors
  • Outdoor beams or perimeter detection where appropriate
  • CCTV cameras and video verification
  • Gate and access-control systems
  • Electric fencing or other perimeter security
  • Alarm communication and monitoring

PIR maintenance checklist

  • Inspect the detector and mounting for damage or movement.
  • Keep the lens clean according to the manufacturer's instructions.
  • Check that furniture or new installations have not changed the detection field.
  • For wireless units, monitor battery status and replace batteries as specified.
  • Investigate repeated alarms instead of simply bypassing the zone.
  • Test the alarm system periodically using the manufacturer's and installer’s procedures.

Choosing the right PIR sensor

Start with the environment rather than the sensor's marketing name. A normal indoor room, a pet-friendly home, a warehouse, a garage and an exposed outdoor perimeter can require very different detection strategies.

Professional tip: If a PIR is repeatedly triggering false alarms, replacing it with another random PIR may not solve the underlying problem. First identify whether the issue is environmental, mechanical, electrical, configuration-related or simply an unsuitable detector type.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is a PIR motion sensor?

A PIR sensor detects changes in infrared energy associated with movement and can report that event to an alarm system.

Can a PIR detect through a wall?

Standard PIR sensors are designed to detect within their specified field of view; they are not normally intended to detect movement through walls.

Can I use a PIR if I have pets?

Some PIRs provide pet-immunity features, but suitability depends on the detector specification, animal and installation. A professional assessment is recommended.

Why does my PIR alarm keep triggering?

Check for environmental movement, heat sources, insects, pets, loose mounting, changed furniture, wiring issues or an unsuitable detector. Repeated faults should be investigated rather than permanently bypassed.

Are outdoor PIR sensors suitable for every perimeter?

No. Outdoor detection is highly environment-dependent. The detector must be appropriate for the conditions and correctly positioned, and other perimeter technologies may be more suitable in some locations.
