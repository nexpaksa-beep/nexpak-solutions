Quick answer: CCTV for a home is most effective when cameras are positioned around the property's important access points and vulnerable areas rather than simply pointing cameras at everything. A well-designed residential system normally considers the main gate, pedestrian entrance, driveway, front door, side access, rear areas and other blind spots. Camera type, image resolution, lighting, night vision, recording storage and remote viewing should all be selected as one system.

Why CCTV is valuable for home security

CCTV gives homeowners a visual record of activity around the property. Unlike a security measure that only reacts to an event, cameras can help you see what happened before, during and after an incident.

A residential CCTV system can support deterrence, incident verification, monitoring of entrances and investigation of suspicious activity. When combined with other security layers, it can become part of a broader security strategy rather than a standalone product.

Visual verification

See what is happening at gates, entrances, driveways and other important areas.

Remote awareness

Compatible systems can let you check live cameras and recorded footage from a mobile device.

Recorded evidence

Recorded footage can help establish what happened during an incident, subject to applicable laws and proper system operation.

Security integration

CCTV can complement electric fencing, alarms, access control and gate automation.

Where should CCTV cameras be installed around a house?

The best camera locations depend on your property layout, sightlines and security objectives. Start with the places where people enter, leave or approach the property.

Main gate and driveway

Cover vehicle and pedestrian approaches where practical. The camera should provide useful identification detail without being aimed directly into glare from headlights.

Pedestrian entrance

A dedicated view of a pedestrian gate or entrance can provide a useful record of people approaching or entering the property.

Front door

Position the camera so visitors can be seen clearly while avoiding an angle that makes faces appear heavily backlit.

Side access

Narrow passages and side gates can become blind spots if the camera plan focuses only on the front of the property.

Rear and vulnerable areas

Consider garages, outbuildings, pool areas, service entrances and other locations that warrant monitoring.

Planning tip: Avoid assuming that one wide-angle camera can replace several correctly positioned views. Coverage, identification detail and lighting all matter.

Which CCTV cameras are suitable for homes?

Different camera designs solve different problems. The right choice depends on the mounting location, required viewing angle, lighting and environmental conditions.

How many CCTV cameras does a home need?

There is no universal camera count. A small property may require only a few strategically placed cameras, while a larger property with multiple gates, a long driveway, outbuildings or perimeter access may need considerably more.

A better approach is to identify every important access route and blind spot first, then determine the camera position and lens needed to produce useful coverage. Counting cameras before planning the views can lead to gaps or unnecessary overlap.

Home CCTV resolution: how much detail do you need?

Higher resolution can provide more image detail, but resolution alone does not guarantee useful footage. Lens selection, lighting, sensor quality, compression, installation height and viewing distance all affect the result.

For example, a camera intended to identify a person at a gate may need a different lens and positioning strategy from a camera intended simply to monitor a broad driveway.

2MP / Full HD

Can be suitable for general monitoring where viewing distances and lighting are favourable.

4MP–5MP

Offers additional detail and can be useful for many residential applications.

8MP / 4K

Provides substantially more image detail but can increase storage and bandwidth requirements.

Lens matters

A suitable focal length can be just as important as resolution when a specific area needs useful identification detail.

Night vision for home CCTV

Many security events occur when natural light is limited, so night performance should be considered before buying cameras. Infrared illumination, low-light sensors, white-light illumination and colour night-vision technologies can produce different results.

At night, nearby walls, reflective surfaces, rain, insects, headlights and security lighting can affect image quality. A professional design should consider the actual scene rather than relying only on a camera's advertised maximum IR distance.

For a deeper explanation, see CCTV Night Vision Explained.

How much CCTV storage does a home need?

Storage requirements depend on the number of cameras, resolution, frame rate, bitrate, compression, recording schedule and desired retention period.

Continuous recording normally uses more storage than motion-based recording. Modern compression can reduce storage requirements, but actual results vary by scene activity and camera settings.

For detailed planning, read our CCTV Storage & Hard Drive Guide.

Can you view home CCTV on a phone?

Yes. Many modern DVR, NVR and IP camera systems support remote viewing through a compatible mobile application. Depending on the system, homeowners may be able to view live cameras, review recorded footage, receive notifications and manage selected settings remotely.

Remote viewing depends on correct network configuration and a reliable internet connection. Account security is also important: use strong unique passwords, keep firmware and applications updated, and enable additional security features where supported.

See our guide to CCTV Remote Viewing on Your Phone.

Integrating CCTV with your home's security system

CCTV is strongest when it works alongside other security layers. For example, an electric fence can protect the perimeter, an alarm can detect an intrusion event, access control can manage entry, and CCTV can provide visual verification.

Electric fencing

Use perimeter protection with CCTV views of likely approach and breach areas.

Alarm systems

Alarm events can be investigated using relevant camera views where compatible systems are integrated.

Access control

Monitor important entrances alongside controlled access points.

Gate automation

Combine gate operation and camera coverage to improve awareness around vehicle entrances.

HD CCTV vs IP CCTV for a home

Both HD-over-coaxial systems and IP systems can be appropriate for residential security. The decision should consider existing cabling, desired resolution, networking, PoE requirements, scalability and the overall system architecture.

What affects the cost of home CCTV?

The final price of a residential CCTV system is influenced by much more than the number of cameras. Camera specification, recorder capacity, storage, cabling, PoE equipment, installation complexity, mounting requirements, network infrastructure and remote-viewing requirements can all affect the project.

Properties with long cable runs, multiple buildings, difficult rooflines or extensive perimeter coverage may require additional equipment and labour. A site-specific assessment is therefore more useful than choosing a system solely by camera count.

For broader pricing guidance, see CCTV Installation Cost South Africa.

Common home CCTV mistakes

  • Choosing cameras only by megapixel count.
  • Installing cameras without checking night-time lighting.
  • Leaving gates and side passages uncovered.
  • Using insufficient storage for the required retention period.
  • Ignoring network and PoE requirements for IP systems.
  • Placing cameras where glare or backlighting reduces useful detail.
  • Assuming a PTZ camera can replace fixed cameras at critical access points.
  • Failing to secure remote-viewing accounts and connected equipment.

Residential CCTV maintenance

Outdoor cameras should be inspected periodically for dust, dirt, cobwebs, water ingress or vegetation affecting the view. Check that cameras remain firmly mounted and that night images are still clear.

It is also sensible to check recorder health, storage capacity, system time, network connectivity and mobile access. Firmware and software should be maintained according to the manufacturer's guidance.

Privacy and responsible CCTV use

Homeowners should consider privacy when positioning cameras, particularly where cameras may capture neighbouring properties, public areas or other people's private spaces. The appropriate legal and privacy obligations depend on the circumstances and applicable South African requirements.

Design cameras to monitor your legitimate security areas while avoiding unnecessary intrusion into spaces outside your control.

Frequently Asked Questions About CCTV for Homes

How many CCTV cameras does a home need?

It depends on the property's layout and security objectives. Start by mapping the main gate, pedestrian entrance, driveway, front door, side access, rear areas and other vulnerable locations, then select camera positions based on useful coverage.

Can I view my home CCTV cameras on my phone?

Yes. Compatible DVRs, NVRs and IP camera systems can support mobile remote viewing when correctly configured and connected to the internet.

What CCTV camera is best for a house?

There is no single best camera for every house. Turret, dome, bullet, PTZ and IP cameras can all be useful depending on location, lighting, viewing distance and the required field of view.

Should home CCTV record continuously?

Continuous recording provides a complete timeline but uses more storage. Motion recording can reduce storage use. The appropriate option depends on your security objectives and retention requirements.

Can CCTV work with an electric fence and alarm?

Yes. CCTV can complement electric fencing and alarm systems and may be integrated with compatible equipment. The exact integration depends on the products and system design.

Is 4K CCTV necessary for a home?

Not always. 4K can provide more detail, but lens choice, lighting, sensor quality and camera placement are also important. A well-designed lower-resolution camera can outperform a poorly positioned high-resolution camera for a specific application.
