CCTV Storage Planning

How Much CCTV Storage Do You Need?

Understand camera count, bitrate, resolution, FPS, recording hours and retention days so you can choose a practical CCTV storage capacity for a home, business, farm or larger security installation.

Request a CCTV QuoteRead the CCTV Guide
Quick answer: CCTV storage is driven primarily by the amount of video data being recorded and how long you need to keep it. Camera resolution alone does not tell you the required hard-drive size. Bitrate, FPS, compression, recording mode and retention period all matter.

Why CCTV Storage Requirements Vary

A four-camera system can require very different storage from another four-camera system. One installation may use lower bitrates and motion recording, while another records higher-resolution video continuously.

For accurate planning, consider these variables:

  • Number of cameras
  • Video bitrate
  • Resolution and compression
  • Frame rate (FPS)
  • Continuous, scheduled or motion recording
  • Hours recorded per day
  • Required retention period
  • Audio, analytics and other recorded streams where applicable

The Basic CCTV Storage Formula

A useful planning method is to estimate the total recording bitrate, convert it to a daily data requirement, then multiply it by the number of retention days.

Approx. storage = total bitrate × recording time × retention period

For example, if several cameras collectively record at a known average bitrate, that bitrate can be converted into gigabytes per day. The result is an estimate, not a guarantee, because actual recording data varies with scene activity and encoder behaviour.

Camera Count Matters

More cameras generally mean more video data. If two systems use comparable camera settings and recording schedules, doubling the camera count will broadly increase the storage requirement in the same direction.

Resolution, FPS and Bitrate

Higher-resolution cameras can capture more detail, but the storage requirement is ultimately governed by the amount of encoded data being recorded. A higher frame rate may also increase data requirements, depending on the camera and codec settings.

Bitrate is therefore one of the most useful values for storage calculations. If your camera or NVR provides a configured bitrate, use that figure rather than guessing from megapixels alone.

Example planning logic

Suppose a system has 8 cameras. If each camera is configured for an average recording bitrate of 4 Mbps, the combined recording stream is approximately 32 Mbps before considering other streams or overhead. You can then convert that total data rate into daily storage and multiply by your desired retention period.

Continuous vs Motion Recording

Continuous recording is predictable and useful when you need a complete record of activity. It also produces a large and steady volume of data.

Motion-based recording can reduce storage consumption because the system records fewer periods. However, busy scenes, moving vegetation, animals, shadows and other activity can trigger recording frequently.

For important security areas, many installations use a combination of recording schedules and appropriate motion/analytics settings rather than relying on motion recording everywhere.

How Many Days Should CCTV Record?

Retention should be based on how long footage needs to remain available for investigation, operational requirements and your organisation's policies. A shop, home and large farm may have very different needs.

There is no universal camera-count rule for a particular hard-drive size because bitrate and retention can change the answer dramatically. Instead, match the drive to the calculated data requirement and the recorder's supported storage capacity.

Use Surveillance-Rated Hard Drives

CCTV recorders can write video continuously for long periods. A surveillance-oriented hard drive is designed for this type of workload and should be selected according to the recorder manufacturer's compatibility guidance.

For larger systems, consider whether the NVR supports multiple drives, the maximum supported capacity, drive health monitoring and the desired redundancy strategy.

CCTV Storage for Homes

For a home, focus on entrances, driveway coverage, gates and other important areas. Decide whether you need continuous recording or whether selected cameras can use motion or event-based recording. A smaller camera count does not automatically mean a small storage requirement if high-bitrate continuous recording is used.

CCTV Storage for Businesses

Businesses often need longer retention and more cameras. Retail entrances, tills, warehouses, parking areas and loading zones may have different recording requirements. Storage planning should be done alongside NVR capacity, network bandwidth and camera bitrate.

CCTV Storage for Farms

Farms may have remote cameras spread over large areas. Wireless bridges or fibre links can affect the overall architecture, while long retention periods can make storage planning especially important. Consider which cameras require continuous recording and which can use event-based recording.

Don't Forget the NVR

Your hard-drive capacity is only one part of the system. Check the NVR's supported camera count, recording resolution, incoming bandwidth, supported HDD capacity and recording features before purchasing storage.

For PoE systems, also consider the network switch or NVR's PoE capacity. See our PoE CCTV guide and CCTV network switch guide.

Storage Planning Checklist

  1. List every camera.
  2. Record each camera's resolution and configured bitrate.
  3. Confirm the recording FPS.
  4. Decide which cameras record continuously.
  5. Decide which cameras can use motion/event recording.
  6. Choose the required retention period.
  7. Calculate the approximate total data requirement.
  8. Check NVR HDD compatibility and maximum capacity.
  9. Allow sensible planning headroom rather than sizing the drive to the absolute minimum.
  10. Review the design as cameras are added or settings change.

Common CCTV Storage Mistakes

  • Choosing an HDD based only on camera megapixels.
  • Ignoring bitrate and recording mode.
  • Forgetting that additional cameras increase the total data rate.
  • Assuming motion recording always produces the same savings.
  • Installing a drive without checking NVR compatibility.
  • Planning for the minimum retention period and leaving no operational headroom.
  • Changing camera settings later without reassessing storage.

Frequently Asked Questions

How much storage does one CCTV camera need?

It depends mainly on bitrate, recording hours, compression, resolution, frame rate and retention period. There is no single storage figure for every camera.

Is 1TB enough for CCTV?

It can be suitable for a small system or shorter retention period, but several high-bitrate cameras recording continuously can consume 1TB relatively quickly.

Does higher FPS use more CCTV storage?

It can. Higher frame rates may increase recorded data, although the actual result depends on the camera's encoding and bitrate configuration.

Does motion recording save storage?

Usually, but the amount saved depends on how much activity the cameras detect. Busy areas can generate frequent events.

Should I use a normal desktop hard drive in my NVR?

Use a storage device appropriate for surveillance recording and compatible with the recorder. Check the NVR manufacturer's supported drives and capacity limits.
