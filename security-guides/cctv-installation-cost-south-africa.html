CCTV Security Guide

CCTV Installation Cost South Africa

How much should a CCTV security camera system cost? This practical guide explains the equipment, installation, storage, cabling and site factors that influence CCTV pricing for homes and businesses.

Request a CCTV Quote

A CCTV quote is more than a camera price

A professional system combines cameras, recording, storage, infrastructure, installation and configuration. The right design is based on what you need to see, where you need to see it and how long you need recordings retained.

How much does CCTV installation cost in South Africa?

The honest answer is: it depends on the system. There is no responsible one-size-fits-all CCTV installation price because two properties with the same number of cameras can require very different equipment and labour.

For an accurate price, the quotation should consider the camera type and resolution, DVR/NVR, storage capacity, cable distances, mounting points, network requirements, power, access to the installation area, remote viewing and any integration with other security systems.

Use online prices as a starting point only. A site-specific quotation is normally more useful because it accounts for the actual property and the required coverage.

What are you paying for?

A CCTV system is made up of several components. The balance between these components has a major effect on the final project cost.

Cameras

Camera type, resolution, lens, night vision, weather protection, analytics and mounting position all influence equipment selection and price.

DVR or NVR

The recorder determines how cameras are connected, recorded, managed and accessed. IP systems generally use NVR-based architecture.

Storage

Hard-drive capacity depends on camera count, resolution, recording settings, compression and the number of days of footage you want to retain.

Cabling & PoE

HD systems commonly use coaxial infrastructure while many IP systems use network cable and PoE equipment. Cable length and routing matter.

Installation

Labour includes mounting, cable routing, termination, camera alignment, recorder setup, testing and commissioning.

Remote Viewing

Mobile access may require recorder configuration, network setup, app configuration and secure remote connectivity.

Typical CCTV system sizes

Camera count is one of the most visible factors in CCTV pricing, but it should not be the only consideration.

System sizeTypical applicationWhat can affect the quotation
2–4 camerasSmall homes, entrances, driveways and limited coverage areas.Camera specification, cable routes, storage and mounting access.
6–8 camerasLarger homes, small offices, shops and multiple external areas.More recording channels, additional cable runs, storage and network requirements.
8–16 camerasBusinesses, warehouses, larger properties and multi-zone coverage.Recorder capacity, larger storage, PoE/network infrastructure and installation complexity.
16+ camerasCommercial, industrial, estate and multi-building environments.System architecture, networking, multiple recording units, fibre or long-distance links and advanced management.

These are system-size categories, not fixed price promises. A four-camera premium IP installation can cost more than a larger basic system depending on equipment and site requirements.

HD CCTV vs IP CCTV: how it affects cost

HD CCTV

HD-over-coax systems can be a practical choice where existing coaxial infrastructure is usable. They can provide strong image quality while keeping system architecture straightforward.

IP CCTV

IP cameras use network infrastructure and can offer high resolutions, PoE, analytics and flexible expansion. Network design becomes an important part of the installation.

Which is cheaper?

There is no universal winner. The most cost-effective choice depends on the property, existing cabling, camera requirements, distances and future expansion plans.

What makes a CCTV installation more expensive?

Long cable runs
Large distances can require more cable, network equipment or different transmission methods.
Difficult access
High walls, ceilings, roofs and restricted areas can increase labour and equipment requirements.
Multiple buildings
Separate buildings may require wireless links, fibre, network infrastructure or additional equipment.
Higher resolution
Higher-resolution cameras can increase equipment and storage requirements.
Longer retention
Keeping more days of footage generally requires additional storage capacity.
Advanced features
PTZ, analytics, licence-plate recognition and other specialised functions can change the system specification.

CCTV storage: an often-overlooked cost

A camera does not simply record forever. Your storage requirement depends on how many cameras you have, their resolution, frame rate, compression, recording mode and how many days of footage you want available.

Example: An eight-camera system recording continuously at higher resolutions for a longer retention period can require substantially more storage than an eight-camera system recording on motion with a shorter retention target.

When requesting a quotation, specify your required retention period if you have one—for example, whether you need several days, a couple of weeks or longer. The installer can then size the storage appropriately rather than simply supplying the cheapest available hard drive.

Residential CCTV installation

For a home, the goal is usually to protect key areas rather than simply install as many cameras as possible.

Entrance & gate

Cover visitors, vehicles and access points while considering the camera's field of view and lighting.

Driveway & perimeter

Position cameras to identify movement and activity around vulnerable approaches to the property.

Back & side areas

These areas are often overlooked and can benefit from dedicated coverage depending on the property's layout.

Commercial CCTV installation

Businesses often need wider coverage, longer retention, better identification and more structured networking than a basic residential system.

Retail

Entrances, tills, aisles, stock rooms and delivery areas can require different camera views.

Warehouses

High mounting positions, large spaces and long cable routes can influence both equipment and labour.

Offices

Entrances, parking, reception and restricted areas can be covered while considering appropriate privacy practices.

Don't choose a CCTV system by camera count alone

A cheap eight-camera system is not necessarily better value than a well-designed four-camera system. The important question is whether the cameras can produce useful evidence where it matters.

Good CCTV design starts with the objective: detection, observation, recognition or identification. Camera placement, lens selection, lighting, mounting height and image resolution should all support that objective.

For example, a wide-angle camera may show that someone entered a driveway, while a correctly positioned camera with an appropriate lens may provide a much better view of the person's face or vehicle registration area.

Frequently asked questions

How much does CCTV installation cost in South Africa?

There is no single price that applies to every property. The final cost depends on camera type and quantity, recorder, storage, cabling, installation difficulty, networking, remote viewing and any additional security integration.

Is IP CCTV more expensive than HD CCTV?

It can be, but not always. IP CCTV can offer features such as PoE, higher resolutions, analytics and flexible networking. Existing infrastructure and the property layout can make one approach more economical than the other.

Does the CCTV installation price include storage?

It should be clearly stated in the quotation. Storage is a major part of a recording system and should be sized according to the required retention period and recording settings.

Can CCTV cameras be viewed from a phone?

Many modern systems support mobile remote viewing. The recorder and network need to be correctly configured and the relevant application set up.

Why can two CCTV quotations with the same number of cameras be very different?

The camera count does not tell the whole story. The camera models, resolution, lens, recorder, storage, cable distances, mounting conditions, networking and installation quality can all differ.

Should I get a site assessment before buying CCTV?

For a professional installation, a site assessment or detailed property discussion is highly recommended. It helps determine coverage, camera positions, cable routes, storage and equipment requirements.
